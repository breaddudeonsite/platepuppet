Camera

Scrub to a time, orbit with the mouse (drag, scroll, right-drag), or tap a framing, then + Key. Keys glide or cut in order. Drag keys on the timeline to retime. Auto-direct on the Music tab does this for you.

The camera travels with the subject and keeps your framing. Camera keys keep their framing relative to where the subject starts. Auto-direct frames the actors itself, so leave this off for that.

Storyline

One scene per line: Scene 2 | verse | Mara walks in, looks around, points at Jo | len=8 shot=close loc=table voice=greeting. Section (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro) sets pacing and camera. Options are all optional: len (seconds), bars, shot (wide, med, close, low, top), loc (left, right, center, back, front, or a prop name), voice (part of a voice's name), env (part of an environment's name, or none), face (camera). Write it like directions: Mara calls out to Jo, Jo turns to look at Mara, Jo replies with a wave. Each comma (or “and Name…”) is a step, played in order. Calling, waving or pointing at someone makes them turn back to you; “looks at the camera” works too. Header lines: look: Noir, floor: wood, sky: dusk, bpm: 120. Look, floor and sky apply to the whole video.

Off (default): each action plays at its natural length and the story ends when it ends. On: scenes and actions are spread out to fill the whole song.



Assets
Everything you've added gets used: leftover voices, motions and props are spread across the scenes. Untick duplicates to leave them out, or reassign a voice to another character, then build again.
3D models (.glb/.gltf) become walkable sets and are scaled to about 16 units wide, adjustable below. A wide 2:1 picture becomes a 360° backdrop; any other picture becomes a flat backdrop.







Cue sheet: direct the actors


Write it like a script, one beat per line, in order. Each line is NAME: command. Start a line with + to make it happen at the same moment as the line before.


go to / run to / strut to / sneak to a mark, a character or x,z  ·  face a character, a mark, or away  ·  wave, think for 3, do any motion by name  ·  say plays that character's next voice clip (or say 2 / say greeting)  ·  wait 2  ·  marks: mark DOOR = -2,1.5

Bring your own AI












Your key goes straight from this page to the provider you pick and nowhere else. If you don't tick remember, it is gone when you close the tab. Any model name works: type it in. Ollama and LM Studio need no key; Ollama must allow this page's origin (OLLAMA_ORIGINS). "Custom" takes any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.


AI director





The offline writer needs no account or internet. It reads your description for a mood (tense, spooky, happy, sad, romantic…), the number of scenes (“8 scenes”), and any character names, then writes a storyline from your cast, props and environments. Click again for a different version.




AI environments
Build set works with any text model: it designs a low-poly terrain with scattered trees, rocks, buildings and so on, plus a matching 360° sky, and adds it as a walkable environment. The middle stays flat and clear for your cast. Paint backdrop asks an image model for a 360° photo-style picture (OpenAI, Gemini, Together, xAI or a custom endpoint), then mirrors it so the seam wraps cleanly. Both land in Story › Assets.


AI assistant
Chat with an assistant that sees your cast, props and timeline and can write stories and cue sheets, set the look, key poses and facial expressions, add props and press play. Ask for changes in plain words. Everything it does can be undone with ↶.






Pose & keyframe by hand


1. Scrub to a time. 2. Grab an orange joint dot and drag it: the limb above it swings to follow (drag a wrist and the arm moves). Or tap a dot and use the rotation rings for fine control. That drops a key at the playhead automatically. Move to another time and pose again; the joint glides between keys. Works on top of any generated motion, or on its own.
